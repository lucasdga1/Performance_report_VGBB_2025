## Objectives
•	Identify the MVP (most valuable player) of the 2025 season

•	Identify the MIP (most improved player) of the 2025 season

•	Identify the best possible lineup for the team in the 2025 season

•	Analyze and reflect on the team’s performance in the 2025 season

## Methodology
•	Collect game statistical data using the mobile app: Basketball Stat Tracker
•	The app synthesizes per-game data and also provides team totals across the season
•	Import the tables as PDF to the PC
•	Import game-by-game tables into Excel to analyze team performance
•	Import the totals table into Excel to analyze individual performances
•	Use Power Query to clean, merge, and process the obtained data
•	Use pivot tables and advanced statistical formulas to achieve the objectives
•	Use calculated metrics to rank athletes and teams analyzed

## Metrics and indicators
•	To determine MIP and MVP, only athletes who participated in at least half plus one (6) of the games were considered

## Metrics used for MIP
•	PPG = Points per game of the athlete
•	REB = Total rebounds of the athlete
•	ASS/TO = Assist-to-turnover ratio. This metric indicates how many assists the player generated for each play in which they lost possession to the other team
•	EFF = Player efficiency. This metric distributes all positive player actions (such as points) against all negative actions (such as fouls or turnovers)
•	+/- = This statistic shows the team’s point differential while the player was on the court. For example, if while the player was on the court, the team led the opponent by 2 points, their +/- is +2
All these indicators were calculated by comparing the player’s first-game data with their season average
The final score was obtained by summing the results of all these indicators
Players were ranked from the highest summed score to the lowest

## Metrics used for MVP
•	PPG = Points per game of the athlete
•	REB = Total rebounds of the athlete
•	ASS/TO = Assist-to-turnover ratio
•	EFF = Player efficiency
•	+/- = Team point differential while the player was on the court
•	TS% (True shooting percentage) = This indicator shows the real efficiency of a player’s shooting, correcting the weight of a 3-point shot and free throws. It is given by the formula: TS%=PTS/2⋅(FGA+(0,44⋅FTA)); where FGA is field goal attempts, and FTA is free throw attempts
•	ORTG (Offensive Rating) = This indicator shows a player’s offensive efficiency. It is calculated as the ratio between points and possessions of that player: ORTG=PTS/(FGA+TO-OR+(0.44*FTA)); where TO is turnovers by the player, and OR is offensive rebounds by the player. Offensive rebounds are subtracted because, in this formula, an offensive rebound represents the continuation of a possession rather than a new possession
Players were ranked for each indicator and assigned a score according to their position. The player with the best PPG received a score of 1, and so on
The final score was obtained by summing the ranking scores across all indicators. The player with the lowest total sum was considered the MVP

## Metrics used to determine the best lineup
Various combinations of 5 players were tested. Combinations were compared according to six indicators to determine their offensive and defensive impact. The six best lineups remained and were ranked from 1 to 6 according to the sum of their averages in each indicator
•	PPG = Points per game of the athlete
•	REB = Average rebounds of the athlete
•	+/- = Team point differential while the player was on the court
•	TS% = True shooting percentage, given by the formula above
•	TO = Average turnovers, indicating how many possessions were lost on average
•	PTS/POSS = Points per possession generated by the athlete, given by the formula:
PTS/POSS= PTS/(FGA+TO-OR+(0,44*FTA))

## Team-level analysis and ranking method
To generate the overall team performance report, averages of several indicators were obtained for all opponents and for the studied team
Teams were scored according to their rankings in each indicator. The best team in an indicator received a score of 1
Scores were summed, and the overall team ranking was determined by this sum. The best team had the lowest total score for being better ranked across most indicators
The studied team was compared with all opponents faced, and also according to indicators against opponents that played 2-3 zone, 3-2 zone, or man-to-man defense. Results were compared to prepare the report

## Team indicators definitions
•	PTS = Team points
•	FG% = Field goal percentage; calculated as (\mathrm{FG%} = \dfrac{FG}{FGA}) where FG are made field goals, and FGA are field goal attempts
•	REB = Team total rebounds
•	TO/POSS = Turnovers per team possession
•	PACE = Measure of team tempo. For an NBA game, this indicator is calculated with 240 total minutes and averages 98.5 possessions per game. For the female teams in analysis, total minutes were adjusted to 160, expecting an average of 65.7 possessions per game. Given by the formula:
•	PACE = (160*(possesssions + opponent’s possessions))/(2*160)
•	OFFRTG (Offensive rating) = Team offensive efficiency per 100 possessions; given by: OFFRTG = (PTS/POSS)*100
•	DEFRTG (Defensive rating) = Team defensive efficiency per 100 possessions; given by: DEFRTG= (PTSopp./POSSopp.)*100; where opp refers to opponent data
•	NETRTG (Net rating) = Net difference between OFFRTG and DEFRTG to assess overall team performance in points; given by: NETRTG=OFFRTG – DEFRTG

## MIP identification report
Based on the calculated metrics and indicators, the athlete who demonstrated the greatest statistical growth was Manuela Lacerda, with a balanced increase across many analyzed metrics
She had an increase of 1 PPG between her first game and her season average, 3 rebounds per game, 7.50 points of efficiency, and 2.40 points in +/-. Her total summed improvement across the analyzed categories was 14.37 points
Notable athletes who came close in the analysis:
•	Isadora Carvalho: increase of +7.13 in +/- and +5.38 in efficiency, resulting in a summed score of 13.68, finishing second
•	Laura Bonadio: third place with a summed score of 12.33, highlighted by an increase of +3.89 PPG
Season totals for Manuela Lacerda: 13 points scored, 31 rebounds, 6 assists, and 34 steals

## MVP identification report
Based on the calculated metrics and indicators, the athlete with the best overall performance in the season was Jasmin Santos with a total classification score of 13, followed by Laura Bonadio with 17
Jasmin stood out for the consistency of her statistics and for achieving an average of 19 points of efficiency and 0.45 ORTG, demonstrating her importance to the team
Laura stood out for averaging 5.9 PPG, with a 36% TS%, and 9.2 REB per game, being one of the team’s pillars

•	Lineup 2: Beatriz, Isadora, Jasmin, Laura, and Manuela Lacerda; notable for high PPG production (18.5) but with a high average of turnovers per game (34.8)

## Team performance report
After compilation, cleaning, and processing of the data, both EAB and the opponent teams were ranked according to their performances in the indicators. The best performances were from Graded and Chapel, which stood out in PPG, TO/POSS, REB, and OFFRTG. This reinforces the general idea that teams with the highest chance of winning are those with higher shot volume, fewer turnovers, more rebounds, and more free-throw attempts
Among the 10 teams analyzed, considering that EAB data are the average of its 10 games played, the school ranked second to last in the sum of indicator rankings, with especially poor performance in TO/POSS, OFFRTG, and DEFRTG. This may indicate that the high number of errors affected both the team’s offensive and defensive performance
In comparison by defense faced, the team performed better overall against man-to-man defense, while against the 2-3 zone, the team showed performance above the general average, only lagging in offensive rebounds. One probable hypothesis for these results is that 50% of the games were against teams defending in a 2-3 zone
Data against man-to-man defense may indicate the team performs better when playing at a faster pace, having achieved a higher average of points in those games (20.5), with better field goal efficiency (25%). For comparison, the average PACE was 76.72, 10 points higher than the expected average possessions for the under-17 female category
The team had a small sample of games, only 10, due to the limited number of female teams available in Brasília in the under-17 category. This affected the comparability of data across defenses faced: 2 games were against man-to-man defense, and 3 against 3-2 zone defense (one of which was the team’s worst performance of the season)
